# Identity and historical age slices

1. Resolve award entries to stable person IDs, aliases, geography and art form. Preserve similar names as separate candidates until documented linkage. For joint awards record every named member; require source-bound reviewed membership before marking an ensemble complete.
2. Attribute each date to the recipient, rather than a relative, teacher or organization in the biography. Bind reviews to source hashes and a short subject-specific excerpt. Keep date precision, source quality and identity confidence separate.
3. Parse exact or partial dates into inclusive intervals. Compute the maximum attainable age from the earliest supported birth and the earlier of the latest death and cutoff. A sufficiently late birth excludes exceeding the reference **by the cutoff**, without predicting eventual lifespan. Approximate dates require a reviewed uncertainty interval.
4. Calculate completed years plus additional calendar days; document leap-birthday convention. A calculated exact age is not independent vital-record validation. A contradictory source invalidates the affected decision until reviewed.
5. Report known-male, sex-unknown and unresolved group blockers separately. Do not classify sex from a name or pronoun. Award-entry screening coverage is not a male-person denominator or a probability that the ranking is correct.

Before a universal rank, check all plausible intervening ages and ties. A lower comparator's position in an exact-date subset does not establish its rank across the award's history. Preserve configurable historical cutoff and distinguish attained-age comparison from memorial discovery.
